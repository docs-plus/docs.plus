# Section changes in heading chat: research

The sources and measurements behind the Section changes RFC. The design, its trade-offs and its open decisions are in the RFC, not here.

The proposal is [rfc.md](rfc.md). The community thread is [Discussion #101, "Document Edit History in Heading Chat"](https://github.com/docs-plus/docs.plus/discussions/101).

All sources were read on 2026-10-01. Most open-source code links point at pinned commits, so their line numbers stay valid. A claim with no label was verified in a primary source: a help page, a changelog, code or a paper. Four labels mark weaker claims:

| Label     | Meaning                                                    |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| reported  | Found only in a secondary source, such as a blog or forum. |
| inferred  | A conclusion drawn from verified facts, not stated.        |
| not found | The researchers looked and found no source.                |
| weak      | A small or synthetic sample. Read it as an example only.   |

## Configuration layers

Eight self-hosted products were studied for settings layers: Mattermost, Rocket.Chat, Discourse, Zulip, Outline, Nextcloud, Gitea and GitLab. The RFC's Settings model rests on these findings.

- Most products use three or four layers: instance, container, person, and sometimes person per container. A container is a team, channel, room, repository or document.
- The instance admin sets availability and the default. The container manager switches a feature for all readers. Each person can override the default.
- One admin value can carry availability, default and lock. Mattermost `CollapsedThreads` takes `disabled`, `default_off`, `default_on` or `always_on`.
- Technical capability lives in env or config files, and policy lives in the admin UI. In Mattermost, an env-set value cannot be changed in the System Console.
- Windows and thresholds start as code constants in Outline, GitLab, Nextcloud and Gitea. Discourse exposes them as settings and has about 1,104 site setting keys.
- Do not write rows that readers will only hide. In Rocket.Chat issue #16931 (2020), hidden join notices were still written and counted.
- System notices should not raise unread counts by default. Zulip marks resolved-topic notices as read, except in followed topics.

## Literature

One bullet per finding. Full references follow the list. "As cited by" and "as reported by" mark a source the research did not read itself.

- A person who returns asks first: "Is anything different since I last looked at the work?" They want a light answer first, then detail on request (Tam and Greenberg, 2006, p587).
- Grouping low-level changes by spatial or time proximity often fails, because the steps of different goals interleave (Tam and Greenberg, 2006, p590).
- Outcome history, the end difference, often matters more than process history, the steps. Full replay is "too much of a good thing" (Tam and Greenberg, 2006, p590-591, p595).
- A rough age cue was judged more useful than an exact timestamp (Tam and Greenberg, 2006, p595).
- The reason for a change mostly has to come from its author. Software cannot infer it (Tam and Greenberg, 2006, p591).
- "Simply making all change information visible will likely overwhelm the end user" (Tam and Greenberg, 2006, p597). The framework was not tested with users.
- In a TeamWave evaluation, users could not tell what changed since their last visit (Steves and others, 2001, as cited by Tam and Greenberg). They built email workarounds.
- Readers care less about changes by long-trusted co-authors and more about changes by others (Neuwirth and others, 1992, as cited by Tam and Greenberg).
- Messages that the sender pushes "presuppose relevance to the recipient". Their content is "not continually available to be browsed" (Dourish and Bellotti, 1992).
- Writers treat sections as territory: "you respect each others sections so you don't go and edit them". Recent writing carries more ownership (Larsen-Ledet and Korsgaard, 2019).
- Co-writers posted in a group chat when they finished a section, "out of respect". They avoided a section while someone worked in it (Larsen-Ledet and Korsgaard, 2019).
- In asynchronous co-writing, more editing of a partner's text went with lower attraction to that partner. In synchronous work, more chat went with higher attraction (Birnholtz, Steinhardt and Pavese, 2013).
- Writers avoided edits that might look negative, and explained their edits (Birnholtz and Ibara, 2012, as reported by Birnholtz and others, 2013).
- Sessions end after about one hour of inactivity across systems, and after 80 minutes for Wikipedia edits. The data are explicit saves (Halfaker and others, 2015).
- The common 30-minute session rule traces to a 1995 browsing study that used 25.5 minutes (Halfaker and others, 2015).
- Push events from low-rate producers, and pull events from high-rate producers at read time. "It is wasteful to materialize lots of events that will later be superseded" (Silberstein and others, 2010).
- Batching phone notifications three times a day improved attention and mood, but raised fear of missing out. Hourly batching did not differ from usual delivery, except for a lower feeling of being interrupted (Fitz and others, 2019, N=237).
- With no notifications at all, anxiety rose (d=0.56). People could still open apps to pull updates at any time (Fitz and others, 2019).
- Participants got 63.5 notifications a day and viewed most within minutes (Pielot, Church and de Oliveira, 2014, 15 users).
- A day with no alerts made 30 volunteers feel less distracted, but also less responsive and less connected (Pielot and Rello, 2017).
- All 20 employees who turned off work email alerts later turned them back on (Iqbal and Horvitz, 2010, as reported by Pielot and Rello).
- "Even a notification that contains important or useful content can cause disruption" (Mehrotra and others, 2016, 20 users).
- Holding notifications until a task breakpoint reduced frustration and reaction time (Iqbal and Bailey, 2008).
- A per-user volume budget cut email volume by 24% and raised the click rate by 31% (Zhao and others, 2018).
- Bots end up "posting so frequently that human developers stop paying attention" in team chat (Erlenhov, Neto and Leitner, 2020).
- In the FunnelBot study, 25 newcomers said that merged bot comments helped them find information (Ribeiro and others, 2022).
- Live typing was disliked. iChat dropped it because "people hated it" (Alfke, 2009, blog post).
- Google Wave's live typing "revealed your textual fumbles to the world" (Manjoo, 2010, news article).
- 46% of August 2023 English Wikipedia edits had no edit summary. For anonymous editors the share was 74% (Edisum, 2024).
- Current change tools show insert and delete operations one by one. A summary can group edits by theme instead (Santosh and others, 2024).
- Edit profiles keep measures of change per document part and per user (Papadopoulou and others, 2006).
- Activity Streams 2.0 allows several actors on one activity: "Sally and Joe offered the Foo object". It defines no way to describe what an Update changed (W3C, 2017).
- Apple paused AI notification summaries for news apps in January 2025, after inaccurate summaries (reported).
- No dedicated empirical study of catching up after an absence was found. One study of catching up in chat (Zhang and Cranshaw, CSCW 2018) was not read.

## Measurements

The measurements come from the local development corpus on an Apple M5 Max, with Bun 1.4.2. They ran on 2026-10-01, with read-only database access. Production runs on amd64, so absolute times do not transfer. Ratios and shares matter more than times.

The corpus held 655 version rows across 225 documents. Only 4 documents that look human-edited had two or more saves. That class comes from a slug and actor heuristic. Most rows came from scripts and test runs. Treat counts as shape, not as production rates (weak).

### Attribution coverage

Method: a walk over two stored snapshots counted the items each Yjs client created per Section, and found removed text in the older snapshot. A client counts as a person only when the server bound it to a signed-in user.
